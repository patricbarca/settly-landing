How to ask friends to pay you back (without it being awkward)

4 min read

You paid for the Airbnb, the tickets or the big dinner, and weeks later you're still waiting. Asking feels petty, so you don't — and resentment builds. Here's how to get paid back without damaging the friendship.

1. Ask early

The best moment is right after the expense, while everyone remembers it. "I'll send everyone their share tonight" is normal; "remember that dinner in March?" is awkward.

2. Be specific and make it easy

Say the exact amount and how to pay: "Your share of the cabin is $142.50 — here's my PayID." Vague requests get vague responses.

3. Let a neutral tool do the reminding

A reminder from an app doesn't feel personal. Settlia shows everyone their balance, sends automatic reminders to those who still owe, and lets you add your payment details (PayPal, Revolut, bank transfer) so paying takes one tap.

Polite messages you can copy

  • "Hey! Settling up the trip — your part is $X. No rush, whenever you can this week 🙂"
  • "Quick one: I've put the dinner in Settlia, you'll see your share there."
  • "Just closing the month's flat expenses — are you OK to send $X?"

Prevent it next time

Log shared expenses as they happen, not at the end. When balances are visible to everyone, people tend to pay without being asked.

FAQ

How do I politely ask a friend for money back?

Ask soon after the expense, give the exact amount and an easy way to pay, and keep the tone light.

What if a friend keeps forgetting to pay?

Use an app that shows balances and sends automatic reminders, so the nudge doesn't come from you personally.

Is it rude to ask for small amounts?

No. Small amounts add up, and settling regularly keeps things fair for everyone.
