How to track personal and shared expenses in one app

6 min read

Most budgeting apps only see your card. Most bill-splitting apps only see your groups. But your real month is both: the coffee you bought alone, plus your share of the flat's rent, the weekend away and last Friday's dinner. If you only look at one side, your numbers are wrong.

Why your card statement lies about your spending

When you pay the whole $120 dinner for four, your bank says you spent $120. You didn't: you spent $30 and lent $90. When a friend pays the Airbnb, your bank says you spent nothing — but you owe your share. Shared costs distort both directions, so a budget built only on card data over- or under-counts every month.

The fix: count your share, not the payment

Your real spending = personal expenses + your share of each group expense. Who paid only matters for who owes whom, not for what you consumed. Once you think this way, "I spent a lot this month" turns into a clear breakdown by category.

Keep personal expenses private

You want one place for everything, but your groups don't need to see your gym, your gifts or your late-night takeaway. A private personal space next to your shared groups gives you the full picture without oversharing.

From tracking to planning

  • Recurring costs: rent, phone, subscriptions — count their real charges each month (a weekly rent hits 4 or 5 times).
  • Estimated monthly spend: the average of the last three months per category is a realistic budget.
  • Income and savings: income minus fixed and variable costs shows what's left this month and where you'll be in a year.

FAQ

What counts as my spending if I paid for the whole group?

Only your share. If you paid a $120 dinner split between four, your real cost is $30; the other $90 is money your friends owe you.

Can people in my groups see my personal expenses?

No. In Settlia your personal expenses live in a private space that only you can see.
